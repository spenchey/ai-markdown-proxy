Used Trucks for Towing in Iowa

Shopping a used truck to pull a trailer? Start with the trailer weight, not the grille. Then match the tow rating, payload and hitch setup to that exact truck.

A used truck is a good towing pick when the exact truck can handle your load and it shows you that with documentation. The exact year, engine, axle ratio, cab, bed and towing package matter more than the name on the hood. Newer Silverado 1500 models can tow from 7,200 to 13,700 pounds. Newer F-150 models can tow from 8,400 to 13,500 pounds. Ram 1500 models can tow up to 11,610 pounds. Used trucks can fall below those benchmark numbers depending on how they were built, so check the VIN before you load.

Which used truck should I buy for towing in Iowa?

Buy the truck whose safe tow rating exceeds your loaded trailer, with payload and hitch capacity left over. A boat to Storm Lake, a small camping trailer to a state park, or a work trailer along gravel roads all ask different things from the same truck. Use the loaded trailer weight and the passenger and cargo weight, then compare both to the truck's tow rating and payload.

On a used truck, the badge is not enough. A Silverado 1500, F-150 or Ram 1500 can be built for light towing or near its class maximum. Look for the owner's manual, trailer tow package, trailer brake controller, axle ratio, tire and brake condition, and any service history. Ask us to walk the numbers with you before you hook up.

Is a Silverado 1500, F-150 or Ram 1500 the better tow truck?

Each can tow. The better one is the one that clears your trailer weight with a safety margin and fits the rest of your week.

Can a used truck tow safely through an Iowa winter?

Yes, but winter makes the pre-trip check more important. Iowa can bring ice, snow, high wind and limited visibility. On a snowy day, give yourself extra following distance and take longer to turn or slow. Iowa driver guidance is to clear your windshield, mirrors, head, tail and trailer lights before driving, and to carry emergency supplies in winter.

For towing, that means inspect the trailer, hitch, safety chains, breakaway wiring, trailer brakes and tires before your first winter trip. Cold weather also changes how trailer batteries, brake controllers and tires behave. Need a used truck or a checkup before a snowstorm? Contact us and our team can help.

How do I check a used truck before towing?

Look at the paperwork and the truck. Match the VIN's towing package and axle configuration before you talk about ratings. On the vehicle, inspect the hitch frame and mounting hardware, wiring and lights, trailer brake controller or controller connector, tires, brakes, radiator, oil and transmission fluid, lift-gate and bed condition, and service history. Wear that is normal on a highway vehicle can be an issue on a trailer rig.

Bring your trailer weight and tell the team what you actually pull. We will help you compare the numbers and make sure the truck is set up for that trip.

Should I buy a half-ton or a three-quarter-ton used truck?

A half-ton can be the right answer for a light trailer or occasional weekend trip. A three-quarter-ton is easier to think about when you tow regularly or pull a heavier trailer through gravel or hills. There is no automatic upgrade in price. Some half-tons tow more than some used three-quarter-tons. The truck's own ratings, packages and condition decide.

To pick one, write the trailer's loaded weight and the cargo and passengers you carry. Then compare it to the truck's payload and tow rating. If the answer is close, step up rather than guess.
